Testing lighting predictions in Sponza

An overly bright cache can hide excessive occlusion. Exposure can hide an indirect contribution that is missing altogether. To test one effect in Sponza, keep the saved camera, lens, materials, lights and display transform fixed. Change one mechanism and predict what should change in the capture.

Separate a finite reference from an exact answer

The path-traced images estimate the same scene with a different algorithm. A 1,024-sample image still contains sampling error, and an eight-bounce limit omits longer paths. More samples reduce sampling error within that truncated model; they do not remove the depth limit, repair a different material model, or reconcile different atmosphere settings. Comparing the 256- and 1,024-sample results helps identify residual reference noise. If the larger accumulation contains the smaller accumulation's samples, the two errors are correlated, so their difference is not an independent confidence interval.

For a pixel or receiver region, distinguish the quantity being compared. A scene-linear radiance difference measures a lighting discrepancy under the engine's stated RGB calibration. A difference between the published PNG values measures a discrepancy after exposure, tone mapping, and display encoding. Both can be useful, but they answer different questions. A highlight compressed by the tone mapper can produce a small PNG difference even when the linear difference is large.

Use the same scale for both sides of a comparison. For an error image, state its sign convention, channel combination, and display range. A per-image minimum/maximum stretch destroys the meaning of a brightness comparison. For a diagnostic atlas, a separately declared logarithmic or false-color transform can reveal structure, provided a reader can recover the original value, validity, and address. A bright preview or a successful decompression does not prove that a resource belongs to the selected camera invocation.

Predict the result of each controlled change

Disable final gathering. Compare directly illuminated stone with the shaded arcade. The primary analytic-light and shadow calculation should remain identifiable while the diffuse interreflection term changes. Dedicated scene reflections and analytic specular highlights have separate controls. Our control also removes the probe-based rough-specular signal, so its image difference is not pure diffuse energy. In composition, this tests which contribution owns which part of the image; it does not mean every shiny object should turn black.

The control sets r.Lumen.ScreenProbeGather=0 while retaining the Lumen GI pipeline and dedicated reflections. In this implementation, that branch returns black diffuse textures and a cleared rough-specular texture. Disabling r.Lumen.DiffuseIndirect.Allow instead can select a different lighting path, including a different treatment of sky illumination; its result cannot be interpreted as subtracting one positive term from the baseline. Before interpreting a console-variable comparison, inspect the source boundary the variable controls.

Disable screen traces. Newly exposed world-trace and cache behavior should agree where both representations have adequate geometry, material, and lighting support. Differences near a silhouette or an off-screen reflector point to a representation or validity decision. The change does not isolate ray-intersection accuracy by itself: an accepted screen hit and a world hit may also get their lighting from different sources. Follow the trace route before assigning the discrepancy to one subsystem.

Change reflection routing while retaining material roughness. Changing the dedicated-reflection threshold tests which estimator supplies the response. Changing roughness at the same time changes the physical scattering problem and confounds that test. Similarly, compare cache shading and hit lighting with the same geometry and camera: material evaluation, directional shading, and cached illumination can differ even when both routes intersect the same triangle. Use the records beside the reflection discussion to identify the selected branch.

Change a light while keeping the camera still. A delayed indirect response can come from surface-cache updates, world-cache updates, probe filtering, or final temporal reconstruction. Record the actual update or render-frame identity, and inspect the intermediate signal that each stage owns. A screenshot request is not itself one simulation update. A sequence of decreasing image differences also does not prove convergence to the ideal transport solution; the renderer may be converging to a biased cached solution.

Read an error in the order the computation produced it

Start with the camera and primary visibility: confirm the receiver's depth, normal, material, and active view rectangle. Then inspect direct-light visibility and its contribution. From there, follow the indirect query through probe owner, direction or directional cell, accepted trace route, hit or cache lookup, radiance scale, and integration weight. Finish with spatial validity and reprojected history. A wrong normal near the start can produce a convincing sequence of internally consistent later buffers, while a history failure at the end can hide correct current-frame lighting.

For a numerical walkthrough, record the full address chain. A resource name alone is not enough when several views or versions share it. Use the scene invocation, producing pass, resource version, mip/slice, stored format, row or element stride, coordinate, and validity state. Decode pre-exposure before comparing quantities from different lighting stores. If a shader-local branch or sample choice was not recorded, label a replay as a declared query and show its inputs. Do not turn a replay that happens to match one output into proof that the dispatched shader selected those same inputs.

The Sponza scene and its provenance

The model is the Khronos Sponza glTF asset at revision 8f492390, imported into Unreal rather than rendered by a separate viewer. The input contains 103 primitives, 262,267 triangles, 25 materials, and 69 texture images. The download record preserves the source-file hashes. Upstream credits and terms are retained in the model attribution and license record; the model's listed Cryengine Limited License is distinct from the CC-BY-4.0 license on its metadocumentation. The source asset bundle comes from the pinned upstream location.

For this scene, each primitive becomes a separate static mesh with a local pivot. Recenter positions around the primitive's source bounding-box center c, preserve the glTF node scale 0.008, import the local geometry, then place the actor at 0.8(c.x, c.z, c.y) centimetres. Unreal's metre-to-centimetre conversion and the node scale give local positions 0.8(p − c), with the same axis swap. Adding the actor translation recovers 0.8p. This keeps the original world geometry while giving each mesh a useful local domain for distance fields and cards. Reusing the original indices, UVs, normals, tangents, and material associations is essential; recreating only the silhouette would change the transport problem. Nanite is disabled for these imported static meshes, so this experiment does not compare Nanite fallback and native ray-tracing geometry.

The saved map is /Game/Labs/SponzaTeaching. The chart added to the map uses six spheres of radius 22 cm, centered at z=60 cm on 40 cm pedestals at x=850 cm. Its three roughness values are 0.08, 0.30, and 0.65, used for both a dielectric and a gold-colored metal. This gives narrow and broad scattering responses without changing the atrium's architecture.
