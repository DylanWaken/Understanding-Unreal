Add the door, plants, and mannequin, then follow their state

The baseline established asset sharing with seven simple cube components. Keep those seven placements and the ceiling RectLight, and add the objects whose contracts we have derived. The extension is saved as /Game/Labs/SceneRepresentationRoom, separate from the original /Game/Labs/LumenRoom, which remains the baseline for the material-buffer and Lumen experiments. Runtime object paths retain the loaded baseline package name. The exported inventory, camera and pose report, and asset and source provenance record the experiment. All new screenshots are 1280 × 720 with fixed exposure EV100 = 4, and they preserve the renderer’s stochastic lighting noise.

The new actors have distinct jobs. A door and its attached indicator inherit rigid motion from a shared hinge. Three small plants share meshes across many instance transforms. A template mannequin carries a changing skeletal pose inside one component. The following captures come from Unreal's renderer, so the plant silhouettes, attachment, and animation are implemented as components in the actual scene.

The additions use three instanced components, two door-assembly mesh components, and one skeletal mesh component. The three instanced components contain thirty placements: three pots, three stems, and twenty-four leaves. So the count is six added primitive-bearing components, not thirty-six actors and not a measured draw count. Sections, passes, culling, and renderer-specific paths determine the work that eventually reaches the GPU. The earlier twelve-tile accounting exercise was a deliberately controlled submission plan, not an estimate for this captured frame.

Move the hinge; keep the door mesh

The hinge is at (−150,200,0) cm. A door 100 cm wide and 200 cm high starts along +X and rotates through +90° yaw toward +Y. Its six-centimeter thickness clears the baseline blocks. The indicator starts at hinge-relative (60,0,150), so its center travels from (−90,200,150) to (−150,260,150). These values match the earlier attachment diagram; the computed example and the captured hierarchy describe the same object.

At the scene boundary, this edit is a transform change that affects bounds, current and previous placement, visibility, and supported secondary representations. The door's indices do not change. The emissive indicator may contribute through supported lighting paths, but its presence creates no point or rect light component; adding one would be a separate scene-service contribution, as the light chapter explains.

Change a pose inside one placed object

The mannequin stands at (65,110,0) cm, in front of the matte block. The two images below sample ThirdPersonWalk at 0.10 s and 0.45 s with the actor at the same location and −90° yaw, which isolates a deformation update from a rigid placement update. The template mesh and animation are actual Unreal assets; the two-bone sleeve strip earlier in the essay is a small arithmetic model of the same operation, not a claim about this asset's particular weights.

A real-time implementation must keep a coherent previous pose and placement when it emits motion. It must also supply bounds that enclose the deformed surface and decide which shadow and ray paths can consume that geometry. Seeing the mannequin in a lit image does not establish that software distance fields or a particular GI cache represent its moving triangles. The baseline distance-field and card captures later in the essay are about the seven rigid cube components only.
