Extend the room until its geometry contract is explicit

We have followed seven cube components through the renderer. Keep the same four-by-five-by-three-meter room, red and green walls, matte block, and glossy block, then ask what changes when we furnish it. Three pots of repeated leaves add instancing, and a mannequin adds a skeleton. A cable, a breakable panel, smoke, and a basin each expose a different contract. The room remains the reference frame, so every new object has to say what data it adds, who updates that data, and which rendering consumers can use it. The result is the proposed Arda compatibility contract; Arda does not yet implement these adapters.

The diagrams keep the room's states apart. The baseline capture contains exactly five enclosing static-mesh surfaces and two static-mesh blocks, plus a separate rectangular light. The captured extension adds the mannequin, three pot instances, three stem instances, twenty-four leaf instances, and the hinged door with its indicator; the original seven placements stay fixed. The specialty fixtures are explicit schematic alternatives or additions. A diagram of a cloth strip, groom, Landscape exhibit, Water body, or volume does not claim that the associated plugin is running in those captures, and the alternatives do not coexist in one crowded room.

1. Conventional static meshes

Start with the matte block we already measured. Its component transform sets its world-space size, and its triangles still come from the shared cube asset. The door in the captured extension keeps the same rigid-surface contract while its parent hinge moves. Translating or rotating either object does not make it a skeletal mesh.

Core adapter target · UStaticMeshComponent. The asset supplies indexed triangle sections, vertex attributes, material slots, bounds, and conventional LOD resources; the component supplies placement and rendering flags. Section and material identity, UV and tangent conventions, current and previous transforms, LOD selection, and culling bounds all have to survive, and the same adapter then provides the appropriate depth, base, shadow, and velocity work. Ray-tracing geometry, distance fields, and card representations are built separately; loading a raster mesh does not create them. Material blending and World Position Offset can change which passes are needed. A rigid object that moves every frame still belongs to this geometry family; “static mesh” describes its asset representation, not an immovable actor. StaticMeshComponent.h

2. Nanite-enabled triangle geometry

For a specialty variant, mount a finely sculpted panel on the back wall and enable Nanite on that asset. Its placement and material role stay fixed while the geometry backend changes. The baseline cube room does not, on its own, demonstrate cluster streaming, and it does not prove that an Arda conventional-mesh importer can consume Nanite resources.

Feature mode · typically UStaticMeshComponent, also the supported instanced, spline, skinned, and collection paths. A conventional static mesh importer still cannot consume Nanite's cluster hierarchy, streaming pages, visibility results, or programmable raster and shading behavior. Arda must implement a compatible virtualized-geometry representation or explicitly choose an available conventional fallback and state the fidelity difference. Displacement, masking, deformation, shadow rendering, and ray tracing each need a separate capability decision. An acceleration structure may use a fallback or another supported Nanite representation, so it does not automatically contain the same triangles selected for the camera. The inspected code has a separate skinned-Nanite gate, so the common claim that Nanite is only for rigid static geometry is already too narrow for this revision. SkinnedMeshComponent.cpp: Nanite feature gate See also Epic's versioned Nanite overview.

3. ISM, HISM, foliage, and landscape grass

The three pots, three stems, and twenty-four leaf placements in the captured room extension are the smallest visible instancing test. They add up to thirty instances across three ISM components, with one shared mesh per component. Moving one leaf changes its instance data without duplicating the leaf mesh. The leaves use ISM and a masked, two-sided foliage material; that material choice does not make their source a FoliageActor. A foliage-authored version of the same planter, or grass generated on the floor exhibit, would test producer contracts that the ISM capture alone cannot establish.

Core adapter target, with feature modes · UInstancedStaticMeshComponent, UHierarchicalInstancedStaticMeshComponent, UFoliageInstancedStaticMeshComponent, and UGrassInstancedStaticMeshComponent. The raw surface is shared; instance transforms, previous transforms, custom data, visibility, and identifiers vary. HISM adds hierarchical organization, while foliage and grass systems own placement and regeneration policies. Stable instance identity and bulk updates are part of the contract, along with per-instance data, culling and LOD behavior, and material access to instance variation. Expanding every instance into a separate duplicated mesh can reproduce a picture, but it loses the memory, update, and GPU-driven behavior that foliage needs at scale. A supported Nanite mesh changes the geometry backend, not the ownership of instance data. Shadow and ray representations must preserve the relevant instances, and masked or displaced vegetation needs consistent bounds and coverage. 4. Skinned meshes, morphs, cloth, and mesh deformers

The room mannequin is the counterexample to moving a whole rigid object. In its walk pose, vertices on the forearm move relative to the torso, so one actor transform cannot describe the surface. The two captured mannequin poses demonstrate a real skeletal component. A facial morph, simulated garment, manually controlled pose, or repeated skinned instance of that mannequin needs a separate feature test; one successful skeletal capture does not establish it.

Core skinning target plus feature modes · USkeletalMeshComponent through USkinnedMeshComponent. Inputs include indexed geometry, bone indices and weights, a pose, optional morph deltas, cloth output, and mesh-deformer results. Deformation order is part of the contract, along with current and previous deformed positions, shading attributes, material sections, and updated bounds. A bind-pose mesh with an actor transform will not do. Velocity and shadow passes need compatible deformation; ray-tracing geometry needs an appropriate build or update, and GI representations can differ in fidelity or update cadence. Nanite skinning is separately gated in this checkout, which does not prove that every morph, cloth, or deformer combination follows that path. Record the specific supported combination and fallback instead of advertising one Boolean “skeletal support.” SkeletalMeshComponent.h SkinnedMeshComponent.cpp: skinned rendering modes

Target the skinned and deformation contract, not the USkeletalMeshComponent class name alone. UPoseableMeshComponent supplies manually controlled poses. UInstancedSkinnedMeshComponent adds instance transforms, animation indices, custom data, and previous transforms, so instancing is not limited to static meshes. In the Chaos Cloth Asset plugin, UChaosClothComponent also derives from USkinnedMeshComponent and accepts cloth or outfit assets. Each producer needs its own pose and deformation handoff; checking the skeletal-component class name does not cover any of them. PoseableMeshComponent.h InstancedSkinnedMeshComponent.h Chaos ClothAsset/ClothComponent.h

5. Landscape heightfields and Nanite Landscape

Place a small raised terrain exhibit on the room floor and leave the original floor cube intact. One variant authors the exhibit as Landscape height and layer tiles; another feeds a Runtime Virtual Texture height producer to Virtual Heightfield Mesh. The indoor fixture compares the two terrain providers without adding a second outdoor scene. Choose its dimensions through the provider's valid grid and scale settings; a Landscape is not an arbitrary mesh patch.

Terrain adapter plus feature mode · ULandscapeComponent and ULandscapeNaniteComponent. Conventional Landscape uses tiled height and weight data, terrain topology and LOD rules, material layers, and hole information. Tile adjacency, coordinate and scale conventions, layer sampling, holes, bounds, and a compatible terrain surface all have to be preserved. Nanite Landscape adds derived Nanite mesh data through a distinct component type, but it does not replace every heightfield use: collision, water-related use, and other systems can still depend on the original terrain representation. Shadows and GI need a representation with the same shape and holes; a flat grid with a color texture is not terrain compatibility. The two component classes and Epic's Nanite Landscape documentation make the coexistence explicit. LandscapeComponent.h LandscapeNaniteComponent.h

A separate plugin provider, UVirtualHeightfieldMeshComponent, renders a heightfield from a Runtime Virtual Texture heightmap. It is a different component from ULandscapeComponent, not a renamed version. The contract covers the virtual-texture producer and its sampling rules, min/max height data, tile and LOD selection, material binding, and the provider's explicit velocity and shadow capabilities. The separate RVT producer in the diagram shows that alternative data source. A static export of the resulting surface is a possible approximation, but it loses live updates to the virtual height field. VirtualHeightfieldMeshComponent.h

6. Spline-deformed meshes and spline data

Run a cable along the green-wall skirting and bend it around the glossy block. The centerline stores the control information; a spline-mesh component supplies a source surface and deforms it along that curve. Move the cable bend, then inspect its main-view silhouette, its shadow, and its previous-frame positions.

Feature mode · USplineMeshComponent; distinguish USplineComponent. A spline mesh deforms a source static mesh using spline parameters, roll, scale, and an axis convention. The work includes evaluating compatible positions and tangents, expanding conservative bounds, and keeping the previous deformation for motion. A spline component stores curve information and can draw a debug visualization, but it does not generate a production cable surface on its own. It derives from UPrimitiveComponent in this source, so inheritance alone does not establish a production geometry contract. Spline visualization settings gate its debug draw relevance. Conventional or Nanite spline rendering, shadows, ray geometry, and Lumen capture each need consistent deformation or an explicit approximation. SplineMeshComponent.h SplineComponent.h SplineComponent.cpp: debug draw relevance

7. Procedural mesh sections

Build the back-wall test panel by submitting its vertex arrays and triangle indices from code. Then add one triangle or move a corner while the rest of the room stays unchanged. Either edit isolates the adapter's section creation, buffer lifetime, bounds update, and material assignment from any particular mesh-generation algorithm.

Plugin scope · UProceduralMeshComponent. Its section-creation API accepts vertex positions, triangle indices, normals, UV sets, colors, and tangents. Mutable sections need validated indices, material assignment, explicit update lifetimes, and recomputed bounds. Collision creation is a separate API request; a collision mesh does not substitute for the rendering buffers. Position-only updates and topology replacements should invalidate different resources where possible. Conventional raster, depth, shadow, velocity, and optional ray geometry need compatible updates, and prebuilt static-mesh distance fields or Nanite pages should not be assumed for arbitrary newly generated triangles. The family covers application-provided geometry, whatever algorithm produced it. ProceduralMeshComponent.h: section data contract

8. Editable dynamic meshes

Use an editable mesh object for the same wall test panel and cut a notch into its lower edge. The new boundary changes topology and can split normal or UV seams, an edit that goes beyond moving four positions in a fixed procedural section. Check the notch in the image and in the shadow, and confirm the updated bound stays conservative and still encloses the surface.

Feature mode · UDynamicMeshComponent through UBaseDynamicMeshComponent. The source representation is FDynamicMesh3 through a mesh object, with attributes and potentially changing topology. The import has to convert or directly maintain compatible render sections, attribute seams, material IDs, and update regions. Unreal exposes different notifications for full mesh changes and for smaller attribute or position changes, which shows why a single “upload mesh again” operation misses useful update semantics. A runtime cut can invalidate index buffers, bounds, acceleration structures, and secondary surface representations together. Raster support does not establish Nanite, distance-field, or GI-cache support for every edit; each representation needs an explicit rebuild or update policy. DynamicMeshComponent.h: mesh representation DynamicMeshComponent.h: update notifications

9. Geometry-cache animation

Hang a short cloth strip from the wall test panel and bake its motion into geometry samples. The renderer can then request the sample at time t without solving cloth simulation or evaluating a skeleton. Unlike the optional live cloth on the mannequin, the positions come from the cache timeline.

Plugin scope · UGeometryCacheComponent. Geometry caches provide time-sampled mesh tracks, transforms, bounds, and applicable motion information; topology can differ according to the cache content and codec. Track and time selection, interpolation rules where supported, sample streaming, materials, and previous-position or motion-vector semantics all belong in the adapter. Treating every sample as a skeletal pose invents a bone model the asset does not contain; displaying only one frame loses the motion the cache stores. Shadows and ray-tracing geometry must update with the chosen sample, and expensive rebuilds or missing secondary GI representations must be reported as capability limits. Alembic can supply content for such a cache, but it is not the runtime component. GeometryCacheComponent.h: track-oriented component GeometryCacheComponent.h: motion-vector controls

10. Chaos Geometry Collections

Replace the wall test panel with a pre-fractured collection. The intact view should resemble the earlier panel; after impact, the visible pieces have independent transforms and newly exposed interior faces. With the object and camera fixed, a single combined mesh cannot represent both states.

Feature mode · UGeometryCollectionComponent. A collection carries mesh pieces, a transform hierarchy, material assignments, and simulation-driven visibility or fracture state. Piece identity and transforms, the relationship between parent clusters and visible pieces, interior-face materials, bounds, and current and previous state must be carried through the adapter. A single combined triangle mesh can reproduce the intact object, but without additional structure it cannot describe the transition to independently moving fragments. Conventional and Nanite collection rendering need separate support. Shadow and ray representations must follow the active pieces; secondary lighting representations may update at a different rate. Chaos simulation can remain a separate producer of state when the compatibility goal is to render its output, but the rendered result must preserve the simulation's piece activation and motion. GeometryCollectionComponent.h

11. BSP/model surfaces and brush visualization

Consider an alternative history for the back wall: construct it with brushes and cut a doorway. The baseline room uses cube components, so this does not reinterpret its measured asset inventory. The test asks whether a cooked model surface with the same wall role can enter Arda without the editor's brush workflow.

Feature mode · UModelComponent; distinguish UBrushComponent. Older or blockout-oriented levels can contain model surfaces derived from BSP or brush operations. If those levels are in scope, the adapter needs the resulting polygon and triangle surfaces, surface material assignments, texture-coordinate mapping, bounds, and visibility semantics. It does not have to replay the editor's entire constructive modeling workflow to render a cooked result. A brush component can also serve editor or volume visualization, so its displayed wireframe is not necessarily the intended production surface. Assess conventional raster compatibility separately from ray tracing, Nanite conversion, and GI data; converting a model to a supported static mesh is an explicit migration path with its own fidelity checks. 12. Niagara and Cascade sprite particles

Put a small effects station in the room's open-front area and emit smoke motes above it. In the sprite version, each particle record becomes a facing quad. Moving the camera shows the orientation rule, and overlapping motes show the sorting and compositing.

Plugin scope · UNiagaraComponent with UNiagaraSpriteRendererProperties. Input records carry positions, sizes, rotations, colors, velocities, and material attributes from CPU or GPU simulation. Compatible particle-buffer bindings, billboard orientation, sorting and bounds, indirect counts where applicable, and the renderer's material-input convention all belong in the adapter. A particle is data in a buffer, not a separate UObject mesh component. Translucent sprites need their own compositing and lighting path; masked sprites have different coverage and shadow behavior. Motion, shadow casting, ray visibility, and GI participation are renderer and material features to negotiate, not consequences of drawing a quad. Matching the simulation output format and the sprite expansion is the rendering contract even when the simulation lives elsewhere. NiagaraComponent.h NiagaraSpriteRendererProperties.h

Legacy Cascade content uses UParticleSystemComponent and its emitter and module data instead of Niagara. Its sprite output has a similar low-level quad form but different simulation, attribute, and sorting conventions, so it needs a separate source adapter. The same applies to its mesh, beam, and ribbon modes described below. Niagara support does not by itself import Cascade assets. ParticleSystemComponent.h ParticleModuleRequired.h

13. Niagara and Cascade ribbons and beams

At the same effects station, replace the smoke motes with a continuous light trail. Ordered points now define a connected strip, so adjacency, width, and UV flow become part of the contract. A ribbon and a beam-like effect can share this generated-surface mechanism with different point arrangements.

Plugin scope · UNiagaraComponent with UNiagaraRibbonRendererProperties. A ribbon uses ordered points, ribbon identifiers, width, tangent and facing data, tessellation, and UV-distribution rules to construct connected surface geometry. Beam templates can use this machinery with an appropriate point-generation arrangement; the renderer does not require a UNiagaraBeamRendererProperties class. Ordering, connectivity, width expansion, interpolation, materials, bounds, and previous-frame behavior also belong to the ribbon adapter. Independent point sprites cannot reproduce a continuous ribbon's silhouette or texture flow. Sorting, translucency, shadow behavior, and the ray or GI representation remain separate choices, especially when a rapidly changing trail's topology or length changes every frame. NiagaraRibbonRendererProperties.h

For legacy Cascade, the source modes include UParticleModuleTypeDataRibbon and UParticleModuleTypeDataBeam2 within UParticleSystemComponent. Their tessellation, connectivity, and material-coordinate conventions need explicit translation. Unlike the Niagara case above, Cascade has a named Beam2 type-data class. The shared graphical forms show what Arda's renderer can reuse; each producer keeps its own contract. ParticleModuleTypeDataRibbon.h ParticleModuleTypeDataBeam2.h

14. Niagara and Cascade mesh particles

At the station, emit small fragments resembling chips from the wall test panel. The fragments have visible thickness and orientation, so mesh resources accompany their particle state. Mesh rendering machinery is reused, but the counts and lifetimes belong to the simulation rather than to the persistent instance list used by the planter.

Plugin scope · UNiagaraComponent with UNiagaraMeshRendererProperties. Mesh particles combine particle attributes with selected mesh resources, material overrides, and renderer-specific scaling and orientation. Arda can reuse mesh and instance machinery, but it must adapt particle-driven counts, selection, bounds, previous transforms, and simulation-buffer ownership. The instanced data source and lifetime differ from a persistent ISM component. Conventional and Nanite-related plugin paths, shadows, and ray visibility need separate identification for the selected renderer and platform. A generic “Niagara supported” label that handles only sprites would silently lose debris meshes; a mesh-only implementation would lose smoke and trails. These three particle entries are distinct renderer modes under one high-level component. NiagaraMeshRendererProperties.h

Cascade's UParticleModuleTypeDataMesh is the legacy mesh-particle producer and needs its own attribute adapter. Niagara has renderer families beyond the three surface forms illustrated here: UNiagaraVolumeRendererProperties connects to heterogeneous volume rendering, while UNiagaraLightRendererProperties and UNiagaraDecalRendererProperties produce scene-service contributions. The three particle surface entries therefore do not exhaust Niagara's renderer outputs. ParticleModuleTypeDataMesh.h NiagaraLightRendererProperties.h NiagaraDecalRendererProperties.h

15. Groom strands, cards, and hair meshes

Give the room mannequin hair as a separate specialty variant. At close range, draw strands; at a chosen LOD, use cards or a coarse hair mesh. In every supported representation, root binding must follow the mannequin's deformation. The captured mannequin poses alone do not demonstrate a Groom asset, strand shading, or hair ray-tracing support.

Plugin scope · UGroomComponent. Strands use curve samples with hair-specific attributes and specialized visibility and shading; cards and meshes use surface geometry that approximates groups of hairs. The selected representation, root binding, deformation and simulation output, LOD transitions, material requirements, and bounds must be carried through the adapter. Treating strand data as an ordinary indexed triangle buffer omits the curve-to-coverage problem and hair scattering. Cards need alpha, coverage, and orientation behavior that matches their textures. Shadows and ray tracing have representation-specific resources, and a successful main-view image does not establish GI behavior. The component carries separate material and resource concepts for strands, cards, and meshes, so compatibility must state which modes are supported. GroomComponent.h: representation-specific materials

16. LiDAR point clouds

Imagine surveying this exact room and keeping measured positions and colors instead of rebuilding its walls as triangle meshes. The illustration uses synthetic samples of the room planes to show the data shape; it is not a real LiDAR capture. Changing the point footprint exposes gaps and overlap that a watertight triangle importer never encounters.

Plugin scope · ULidarPointCloudComponent. Raw points carry positions and appearance data, along with spatial organization and point-rendering controls. The contract covers point selection and streaming, size or footprint interpretation, color mapping, camera behavior, and bounds. A small billboard or splat per selected point does not imply that the source contains a watertight triangle mesh. A reconstructed triangle proxy made for another purpose is a separate representation. Shadow casting, ray intersections, and GI coverage must be implemented or approximated deliberately, and gaps between samples and point-size choices change visible coverage. 17. Sparse heterogeneous volumes: smoke, fire, and fog

Replace the station's smoke sprites with a density field. Looking through the same bounded region now accumulates scattering, absorption, and emission along a ray. The cube around the field limits the work; it is not the smoke's opaque skin.

Feature mode · UHeterogeneousVolumeComponent, with sparse-volume assets/materials as applicable. Inputs describe spatial fields such as density, scattering, absorption, and emission, possibly animated through Sparse Volume Texture frames. Volume bounds and transforms, field sampling and streaming, time selection, a step and integration policy, volume-domain material evaluation, and appropriate light and shadow interaction all belong to the contract. A cube can bound the work, but shading only its faces cannot reproduce the smoke. Ordinary surface GBuffer, triangle BLAS, and card-capture support do not imply volume support. The component's resolution, frame transform, and animation fields expose this different data model; Epic's volume documentation covers the renderer-specific path. HeterogeneousVolumeComponent.h

Niagara can also produce this renderer family through UNiagaraVolumeRendererProperties, whose UseHeterogeneousVolumes() returns true. In the inspected header its source mode is Emitter, and particle source mode is explicitly left as future work, which does not show that arbitrary per-particle volumes are supported. The adapter also needs that emitter-level volume's material bindings, transform, size and resolution, and sampling parameters, beyond the volume consumer itself. 18. Volumetric cloud layers

For this service test only, cut an optional skylight into the room's ceiling and look up from the same interior. Clouds belong to the external atmosphere visible through that opening. Scaling a planetary cloud layer down into the room would change the model under test; indoor localized smoke belongs to the preceding volume family. The baseline room has a closed ceiling and no cloud contribution.

Feature mode · UVolumetricCloudComponent, which derives from USceneComponent. Cloud layer altitude and height, a volume material, tracing limits, and lighting and shadow settings describe the rendered medium. A cloud and atmosphere adapter needs compatible density coordinates, ray integration, aerial perspective, temporal treatment, and light interaction. Enumerating UPrimitiveComponent objects will not find these. A sky-textured sphere is an explicitly different approximation. Main-view, reflection, and shadow ray sampling have separate controls in the component, so one generic triangle-pass capability flag cannot describe cloud compatibility. VolumetricCloudComponent.h: layer and sampling settings

19. Paper2D sprites, flipbooks, and tile maps

Mount a small control display on the green wall. One implementation uses a Paper2D status sprite, an animated flipbook icon, and tile-map cells for a small layout diagram. Everything stays planar, but the importer must preserve three different sources of UVs, frame selection, and ordering.

Plugin scope · UPaperSpriteComponent, UPaperFlipbookComponent, and UPaperTileMapComponent. Sprites provide planar render geometry and texture regions; flipbooks choose sprite frames over time; tile maps arrange cells into layers with per-tile appearance and transforms. Pivots, pixel-to-world scale, UV and atlas handling, frame timing, layer ordering, material and blend behavior, and bounds all belong to the contract. Flattening a tile map into a photograph loses parallax and editable layer semantics, while treating a flipbook as one permanent quad texture loses its animation contract. Depth, shadow, and lighting behavior depend on the chosen materials and renderer settings. These surfaces may share low-level triangle code, but they still need different source adapters. 20. Billboards and material billboards

Attach floating markers to the matte block and glossy block. As the room camera moves, their surfaces turn toward the viewer. These are deliberate in-game labels, and editor light icons or component gizmos should not be exported just because they also use billboards.

Feature mode · UBillboardComponent and UMaterialBillboardComponent. A billboard describes a facing quad or similar generated surface, along with its texture or material, sizing, and potentially distance-dependent appearance. Camera-facing orientation, screen and world size interpretation, bounds, and depth and translucency behavior all have to be reproduced. A fixed world plane loses that behavior, because the silhouette changes differently as the camera moves. Some uses are editor icons and should be excluded from a production-scene export according to visibility flags; others are intentional in-game visuals. Shadow, ray, and GI inclusion must follow the content's role and explicit capability; do not manufacture solid geometry from every icon. BillboardComponent.h MaterialBillboardComponent.h

21. World-space text

Place the label ROOM 01 next to the control display. Changing it to ROOM 100 can change glyph count, layout, geometry, and bounds while the component transform stays fixed. A font texture and a transform therefore cannot reproduce the component.

Feature mode · UTextRenderComponent. Text content, font metrics and atlas data, alignment, world scale, color, and material settings determine the generated glyph surfaces. Compatible text layout and glyph sampling matter as much as rendering; the component transform and font texture alone are not enough. Text changes can change geometry and bounds. Depth, masking or translucency, and material lighting determine its pass behavior. Ray and GI treatment should be scoped explicitly; a distant label does not automatically need a solid volumetric proxy. This is world-space scene text, separate from a screen-space UI overlay or an arbitrary rich UMG widget composition. TextRenderComponent.h

22. World-space widget surfaces

Keep the control display's location but implement it as a UMG widget rendered to a target. Compare a flat panel with the component's cylindrical mode. The UI producer supplies the image and its redraw timing; the world-space surface supplies geometry, depth, and transform behavior.

Feature mode · UWidgetComponent. In world space, the widget is rendered to a target and displayed on a configured geometric surface; the component exposes plane and cylinder geometry modes, draw size, and render-target access. Compatible widget evaluation, or an explicitly supplied rendered UI surface, must be paired with the correct transform, curvature, material, alpha handling, timing, and bounds. Screen-space mode belongs to a different composition path and must not be exported as an arbitrary world quad. Interaction and hit testing are a separate compatibility concern beyond drawing. Shadows and GI depend on the surface and material policy; keeping UI readable usually takes deliberate treatment rather than ordinary lit opaque shading. 23. Water bodies and their generated surfaces

Place a shallow basin near the glossy block. Its shell can remain an ordinary static mesh; the optional Water-plugin fixture supplies the water-body description and a generated or custom surface. A blue plane or a material with a water shading model does not establish Water-body compatibility. The basin is a controlled small test, not a claim that all river or ocean features have been exercised.

Plugin scope · UWaterBodyComponent and the Water plugin's mesh-producing components. Water bodies carry shape, material, and water-specific state and can contribute to the shared water mesh or use alternative primitive or static-mesh components. The resulting surface topology and LOD, wave displacement, bounds, materials, depth and underwater semantics, and relevant scene textures all have to be represented. Importing only the basin boundary or a water-body spline does not reproduce the rendered water. Reflection, refraction, shadowing, and GI behavior are specialized material and rendering features to negotiate; a blue opaque mesh is only an explicit fallback. The source exposes both whether a body affects the water mesh and a list of alternative primitive components, which makes the description and geometry split visible. 24. Scene contributors that are not ordinary geometry

Return to the ceiling RectLight from the baseline: it illuminates all suitable room surfaces without adding another cube mesh. Now propose a decal on the matte block, a bounded fog patch near the effects station, or a changed view color grade. Each changes the image through a path that a primitive-component walk would miss.

Separate scene services · UDecalComponent, ULightComponentBase and derived lights, USkyAtmosphereComponent, and UPostProcessComponent. These derive from USceneComponent in the inspected hierarchy, not from the ordinary mesh or primitive path. Decals project material effects onto eligible receivers; lights supply illumination; atmosphere supplies participating-medium behavior; post-process components supply view settings. Arda needs matching service adapters, receiver and pass rules, and blending and priority semantics. Their gizmos and volume bounds are not the final visual effect. Separately, UShapeComponent descendants such as collision boxes, spheres, and capsules, along with brush volumes and spline debug lines, can derive from primitive classes while serving collision or visualization. Fog needs its own service adapter. UExponentialHeightFogComponent describes an exponential height distribution and can enable a distinct volumetric-fog mode; ULocalFogVolumeComponent supplies bounded radial and height extinction and scattering. Both derive from USceneComponent. Neither is equivalent to importing a Sparse Volume Texture into UHeterogeneousVolumeComponent. Density distributions, transforms, lighting and composition rules, and feature settings all have to survive; a triangle shadow or a Lumen card cannot stand in for participating-medium integration. Niagara light and decal renderer outputs must feed the corresponding services while respecting their simulation and source-mode gates. Each room experiment suggests the same import result: keep the component's source family and feature modes, identify the renderable representation, and record which consumers can use it. A mesh can be supported for opaque raster and shadows while its animated ray geometry or GI proxy remains unsupported. A volume can be supported through integration while having no triangle representation at all. Compatibility should therefore be reported as family × feature mode × renderer consumer, with an explicit approximation or rejection for missing combinations.

For Arda, the first milestone can reproduce the baseline room and its captured extension: indexed meshes, the planter's instances, the mannequin's skinning, the hinged door's transform hierarchy, and material and light contracts. Then add the wall panel, floor exhibit, effects station, and control display through stable interfaces for mutable geometry, curves and particles, volumes, and scene services. The order does not permit the loader to discard later families silently. The importer should report them with component names and concrete unsupported modes. Once the adapters exist, the same shared scene identity and update machinery can feed depth, surface shading, shadows, ray tracing, and GI without pretending that all five consumers need identical geometry.
