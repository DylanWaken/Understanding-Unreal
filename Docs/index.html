Unreal Engine rendering, explained from first principles

Understand what Unreal actually does.

Four independent, long-form studies follow real data through Unreal Engine 5.8.1: scene representation, virtual geometry, indirect lighting and material compilation. Each essay combines source-guided explanation, reproducible measurements, original diagrams and unedited engine captures.

The essays

Every article stands on its own. Read them in order for one connected picture of the renderer, or follow a single subject from its first principle to its engine implementation.

Scene representation

Scene Representation in Unreal Engine

Follow one indoor room from shared geometry and placed components through render proxies, GPU Scene records, animation, visible surfaces and lighting representations. Instanced plants, a skeletal mannequin and a hinged door extend the same room into the geometry families a renderer must support.

Virtual geometry

Nanite: From a Scanned Bunny to Visible Pixels

Build a measured cluster hierarchy from the Stanford bunny, select compatible detail, stream pages, test occlusion and rasterize visibility. A computed reference experiment is compared with actual Unreal Nanite builds and captures.

Global illumination

Lumen: From the Path Integral to a Frame

Derive the path integral and a working path tracer, identify Lumen's real-time approximations, then inspect deferred shading, Virtual Shadow Maps, surface caches, probes and reflections in an actual Unreal frame.

Inspect the captured frame: exact texels, packed fields, volume slices, pass parameters and producer versions.

Material compilation

How Unreal Compiles a Material Graph

Trace an expression graph from the Material Editor through analysis, HLSL translation, shader generation, permutation management, caching and the GPU programs that shade a surface.

Source, evidence and reproduction

Source-guided

Claims about Unreal behaviour are anchored to inspected engine ranges at commit 71fe36aac5a8. Historical presentations and screenshots are identified in their captions, and engine source links require access to Epic's Unreal Engine repository.

Reproducible laboratories

The Nanite, Lumen and Unreal scene laboratories publish their authored generators, scene descriptions, evidence records and frozen output hashes. validate_labs.py checks the frozen evidence without a GPU or scientific Python stack.

Offline mathematics

Equations are pre-rendered to static HTML and MathML using a vendored KaTeX toolchain and locally served fonts. Reading requires no content delivery network; the checked-in cache makes the build deterministic.

Using this site

Read the argument

Each essay opens with a problem statement and builds its representation, algorithm and cost model in numbered chapters. Chapter navigation, reading position and a dark appearance are progressive enhancements.

Check the evidence

Figures link to full-size originals, tables preserve recorded measurements, and source lists identify the inspected implementation ranges. The migration record documents exactly what was copied and what was excluded.

Resolve terminology

The graphics glossary gives every linked term a stable address. Without JavaScript the links still work; with it, focus or hover also reveals a concise definition.
